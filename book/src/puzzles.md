# Chess puzzles

Solrac trains you with puzzles from Lichess (free to use, CC0), sorted by theme and rating. Its
starter set holds 83,573: every theme and opening at every difficulty, and Lichess's puzzles of
the day. They run on your own computer: nothing is sent anywhere to pick or check a puzzle.

## Setting them up

The first time you start Solrac it asks whether to set up chess puzzles. Choose **Download** and
Solrac downloads a 4 MB file once, shown on the loading screen with the usual **Continue**
button, then takes a few seconds to prepare it while you play. A small window at the lower left
shows how far it has got, with **Cancel**. You can drag it out of the way. It needs about 60 MB
of free disk space, and Solrac says so before it starts.

Choose **Not now** and nothing is downloaded; Solrac doesn't ask again at start-up. To set the
puzzles up later, choose **Game → Chess Puzzles…** and the same question comes back. If you
close Solrac in the middle, it carries on the next time you start it and says it is resuming.
If something goes wrong, the window says what, with **Try again**.

If an earlier Solrac set up the full puzzle database (6.2 million puzzles, about 3.3 GB), Solrac
removes it the next time it starts and sets up the starter set in its place, without asking
again. The window at the lower left says so. Your record of the puzzles you have solved stays.

## More puzzles as you play

With a licence, Solrac adds puzzles while you train. When the theme (or opening) and difficulty
you picked has fewer than about 200 puzzles you haven't played, Solrac downloads a pack of up to
25,000 more puzzles at that difficulty in the background, about 1.4 MB, and prepares it in a
couple of seconds. The picker says "Adding more puzzles rated 1600–1999…" meanwhile, and its
counts grow once the pack is in. Choosing such a theme brings one pack; another comes for it only
after you have played one of its puzzles (solved, or with the solution shown; a skipped puzzle
doesn't count), so packs follow your play, not your browsing. A puzzle you have played counts as
played whether you solved it or not. One pack comes at a time.

Packs stop at the **puzzle storage limit** you choose in Settings, on the Game page: 1 GB to
start, which is room for the starter set and about 70 packs (see [Settings](preferences.md)).
When the limit is reached, or Solrac already has every puzzle it offers at that difficulty, the
picker says so and offers the nearest difficulty with more puzzles left, and, at the limit,
**Settings…** to choose a larger one. Solrac never deletes puzzles to make room, so lowering
the limit only stops new downloads.

Without a licence, or offline, you train with the starter set.

## Choosing a puzzle

Choose **Game → Chess Puzzles…** to open the picker over the board. Drag it by its title to move
it, or by an edge to make it larger: a taller picker shows more of the theme list.

- **Today's puzzle** is one of Lichess's puzzles of the day, the same for everyone on a date.
- **Practise** groups the themes: motifs (forks, pins, sacrifices…), checkmates (mate in 2,
  back rank mate…), endgames, the phase of the game, openings (by family: Sicilian Defense,
  Italian Game…), or any theme. Each theme shows how many
  puzzles it has at the difficulty you chose; a theme with too few puzzles there is left out.
- **Difficulty** is Beginner (under 1200), Club (1200–1599), Strong (1600–1999), Expert (2000
  and up), or any. These are Lichess ratings, and they run a little different from a chess
  club's.

Press **Start** and Solrac picks a random puzzle of that kind. The picker opens with your last choice. Puzzles whose rating Lichess's
own numbers don't support are left out.

## Solving one

The puzzle is set up on the board, 2D or 3D, turned to your side. The opponent's first move
plays, and then it is your turn: move as you would in a game. A right move is answered by the
opponent. A wrong move is taken back and counted.

- **Hint** marks the piece to move in red on the board, and says which square it is on.
- **Show solution** plays the line out. The puzzle then counts as missed.
- **Skip** finds another puzzle.
- **Leave puzzles** puts your own game back on the board, exactly as you left it.

While a puzzle is up there is no chat beside the board, since it would give the answer away, and
no "Game over" window or camera close-up when a puzzle ends in mate. Puzzles are never saved in
your game library.

When you finish, the card says whether it was a clean solve, and offers **Next puzzle** (the
same theme and difficulty), **Try again** (the same puzzle) or **Back to picker**. The picker keeps how many you solved and how
many days in a row you solved one. A puzzle solved after a wrong move, or with the solution shown,
doesn't count as solved.
